## Find the right gloves

Stretchy nitrile-dipped work gloves may work best, but the right glove varies from person to person. Try a few styles and sizes.

Look for a glove that passes all of these checks:

- **Snug fingertips.** The fingertip holds the tactor against the pad of your finger. A loose glove lets the tactor drift off the skin, and you won't feel the therapy.
- **Comfortable for a full session.** The glove must not restrict circulation or be uncomfortable.
- **Easy on and off.** The glove goes on and off almost as easily as a glove with no tactors, so you can slip it off whenever you need your bare hands.

If you're between sizes, size down. If a glove feels right but the tactor still shifts, you can fasten it. See [Secure the tactor](#secure-the-tactor).

### Test the fit with a tactor

A finished tactor from [stage 3](/docs/tactors) is the best way to test a glove. Hardware and home-improvement stores carry many styles, so take a tactor with you and compare them. Don't cut or mark a glove until you've chosen it, because a store may not take it back.

1. Slide the tactor down inside the glove finger to the tip, with the finger cup toward the pad of the finger. Long needle-nose pliers help you reach it. Grip the tactor gently, and don't pull on the wire.
2. Put the glove on. Your fingertip should rest in the finger cup, with the pin touching the skin.
3. Curl and straighten your finger. The tactor should stay in place, and your fingertip shouldn't feel squeezed or numb.
4. Take the glove off and put it back on. It should move easily over your hand, and the tactor should stay in the fingertip.
5. Take the tactor out and try the next glove.

To feel the buzz before you decide, buy a few gloves from a store with easy returns and test them at home. If the wire lead reaches past the cuff, plug it into a board and follow the [buzz-check](/docs/tactors#buzz-check) steps with the glove on. Return the gloves you don't use.

## Label the gloves

Label the left-hand glove and the right-hand glove with tape. Write down which board, primary or secondary, goes with the left-hand glove and which goes with the right-hand glove. If you mix them up later, swap the roles in the Updater. See [Swap which glove is primary](/docs/updater/firmware#swap-which-glove-is-primary).

## Place each tactor

The tactor sits snugly in the glove's fingertip. Its wire lead runs back along the inside of the finger. The lead's JST PH (2 mm) connector goes out through a tiny hole cut in the glove between the fingers, near the hand and finger webbing, and plugs into the board later.

Do this for each finger. Any gap between the fingers works for any lead, and 2 leads can share a hole. Check the mark before you cut. A hole can't be uncut.

1. Put the glove on and mark the spot between the fingers, near the webbing where the finger meets the hand.
2. Take the glove off. On the cutting mat, cut a tiny hole at the mark. Make it just big enough for the JST PH connector on the wire lead to pass through.
3. From inside the glove, feed the JST connector out through the hole.
4. Slide the tactor down inside the finger to the tip, with the finger cup toward the pad of the finger. Pull the slack wire out through the hole.
5. Put the glove on. Your fingertip should rest in the finger cup, with the pin touching the skin. Keep the whole tactor inside the glove, snug in the fingertip.

## Secure the tactor

A snug glove fingertip holds the tactor by friction. Check it often. If it shifts or drifts off the fingertip, use a snugger glove or a smaller size, or fasten the tactor to the glove.

The slots on the sides of the tactor shell are made for this. Small zip ties fit through them, and so does anything similar. Keep the fit comfortable, and never tight enough to restrict circulation.

## Add a thumb tactor (optional)

The standard build has no thumb tactor. If you add one, don't copy a finger fit.

- The thumb is shorter and wider, and its pad sits at a different angle. Check the tactor position on the thumb before you cut, so the finger cup sits flush.

## Leave the wires loose

Leave enough slack inside each finger for it to curl fully without pulling on the tactor. Don't route the wires yet. You route them in [stage 5](/docs/wiring#route-the-wires), after you test each tactor.
