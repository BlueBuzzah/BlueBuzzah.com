Open-source vibrotactile therapy for Parkinson's

BlueBuzzah gloves send gentle, timed pulses to your fingertips. Research suggests this may help with some movement symptoms. Everything is open, so you can build it, read it and change it.

How it works Build a pair

How it works

  1. Build your gloves

    Follow the open build guide to put together a pair of gloves. Each fingertip gets a small vibration motor.

  2. Load the firmware

    Plug each glove into your computer. BlueBuzzah Updater loads the firmware and sets which glove is left and which is right.

  3. Start a session

    The gloves pulse each fingertip in a changing order. The BuzzahBuddy phone app, coming soon, will let you start, stop and adjust sessions.

The science behind it

BlueBuzzah is based on research into vibrotactile coordinated reset, or vCR. vCR sends short pulses to the fingertips in a changing order. Researchers think this may calm the overactive brain signals linked to Parkinson's movement symptoms.

The studies so far are small and early. People in those studies reported easier movement, but this is not proof that vCR works for everyone.

  • NEURAL REGENERATION RESEARCH · 2021

    Vibrotactile Coordinated Reset Stimulation for the Treatment of Parkinson's Disease

    Tass PA, Stanford University School of Medicine

    A review of vCR research so far. People in early studies reported fewer movement symptoms. Larger studies are still needed.

  • FRONTIERS IN PHYSIOLOGY · 2021

    Coordinated Reset Vibrotactile Stimulation Induces Sustained Cumulative Benefits in Parkinson's Disease

    Pfeifer KJ, Kromer JA, Cook AJ, et al.

    A small study that followed people for 6 months. People reported easier movement over 3 months that lasted to the 6-month check-in. Brain recordings also changed.

BlueBuzzah is an experiment

BlueBuzzah is an experimental, open-source project. It is not FDA-approved and is not a substitute for medical care. Talk with your neurologist before using it.

The four parts

  • Hardware

    In progress

    The gloves: a vibration motor for each fingertip, a circuit board and 3D-printable parts. The battery lasts 8 hours or more.

    Build guide

  • Firmware

    The code that runs on the gloves. It keeps the left and right glove in step and lets you adjust the pulse pattern.

    Firmware docs

  • BlueBuzzah Updater

    A desktop app for macOS and Windows. It loads new firmware onto your gloves and sets them up.

    Download

  • BuzzahBuddy

    Coming soon

    A phone app for iPhone and Android to start, stop and adjust sessions.

    Visit BuzzahBuddy.com

If you have an implanted device

If you have deep brain stimulation (DBS) electrodes, a pacemaker or another implanted electronic device, talk with your doctor before using BlueBuzzah.
